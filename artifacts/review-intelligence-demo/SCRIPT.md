# Review Intelligence — owner demo

## Director's treatment

- **Promise:** A restaurant owner can turn scattered guest feedback into a clear next action without reading every review one at a time.
- **Identity:** The selected App Demo Showcase template: light studio fog `#EEF2F6`, dotted depth field, ink `#0F172A`, violet `#7C3AED`, white browser surfaces. Sora display typography; Inter interface labels.
- **Hero:** A fictional multi-location restaurant review dashboard, always labeled as an illustrative demo with synthetic data.
- **Motion:** Review cards physically sort into theme lanes; a violet alert line isolates a rising complaint; a close camera move pulls out from actionable UI and lands on the weekly owner brief.
- **Arc:** Human-scale overload → structured overview → useful signals → manager-reviewed action → concise owner takeaway.
- **Payoff:** The busy dashboard condenses to a single weekly decision statement, then the final violet signal becomes the opening review card on the loop.
- **Format/runtime:** 16:9, exactly 120 seconds. Silent by user approval; every key explanation appears on screen.
- **Truth boundary:** Every restaurant/location/person/review/data point is fictional. All sample counts and charts are illustrative, not measured product results. No Yelp data, real guest text, identities, or impact claims.

All times are exact on the 120-second runtime clock. The product interface is illustrative artwork; these are not product screenshots or measured results. No narration is requested.

### Shot 01 / 00.00–10.00 / 10.00s — “A hundred small signals.”
- **Purpose:** Make the problem visceral: owners receive reviews one at a time, not as a strategy.
- **Exact content:** 00.00 “A hundred small signals.”; 00.80 “One review at a time.”; 04.20 “What’s changing?”; persistent lower-right “ILLUSTRATIVE DEMO · SYNTHETIC SAMPLE DATA”.
- **Composition / artistic idea:** Fog backdrop and dotted grid. Three oversized fictional review slips enter at different depths, then a stream of smaller slips collects beyond frame. Individual violet, amber, and slate marks carry the eye; no real guest wording is shown.
- **Timed choreography:** 00.00–00.50 headline snaps into view beside a small review card already partly in frame. 00.60, 01.10, 01.60 cards arrive in stiff spring beats. 02.20–04.00 slips multiply into a shallow diagonal stack. 04.20 the question replaces the headline while the cards pause. 06.00 a single violet theme marker pulls free; 08.40 it expands toward the next shot's browser frame.
- **Transition contract:** The violet tag expands into the browser app's active filter pill. The incoming frame overlaps the outgoing card by ~250ms with scale-through and a vertical reveal.
- **Assets/technique:** CSS review-slip UI, local Sora/Inter typography, Framer Motion scale/opacity/clip-path. No real reviews or image assets.
- **Sound intent:** Silent. No music, VO, or SFX.

## Pre-code review

The sequence alternates macro card accumulation, browser-wide overview, a dense theme-board, tight trend analytics, human-centered name slips, a two-pane drafting workflow, group comparison, editorial memo, and minimal lockup. Adjacent beats change scale/composition/visual mechanic. Each scene contains a post-entrance development and outgoing carrier motion; the last 15–20% is handoff rather than a dead tail. There are no real review identities, texts, locations, platform data, performance claims, or quantified business impacts.
